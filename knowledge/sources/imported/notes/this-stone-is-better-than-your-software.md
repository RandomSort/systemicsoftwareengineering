# This rock is better than your software

## Core argument

We talk constantly about software quality — or more accurately, about its
absence — and we keep hearing that quality is a key enabler of speed. But what
*is* quality for software, "the world of things that do not really exist"?
The talk's provocation: a rock can be trusted to behave as expected; the
average piece of software cannot. Despite automated tests, deployments,
agile, continuous delivery, and DevOps, it is not obvious the software got
*better*. Quality needs a definition that survives intangibility, and the
candidate is warranted trust in expected behavior.

## Extracted claims

- Definitional: software quality is best understood as *warranted trust that
  the artifact behaves as expected* — the property the rock has and software
  lacks.
- Empirical (provocation, to be tested): the adoption of tests, CD, agile,
  and DevOps has not demonstrably made the average piece of software more
  trustworthy.
- Definitional: quality talk in industry is mostly *lack-of-quality* talk —
  the concept is defined negatively, by complaint, which blocks reasoning
  about it.
- Normative: teams need explicit techniques for *reasoning about* the quality
  of their software, not just practices assumed to produce it.
- Received claim, examined: "quality is a key enabler for speed — at least
  that is what we keep hearing" (the talk holds this at arm's length rather
  than asserting it).

## Agreements and disagreements

Feeds a trust-centered definition of quality for the framework — quality as a
relation (trust between artifact and its dependents) rather than an intrinsic
attribute. Note the internal tension: DORA-style research asserts
quality-enables-speed as established; this source deliberately keeps it as
hearsay. That gap — practices adopted vs. trust produced — is itself a
framework-worthy distinction.

## Quotable

- "I have trust in a rock to behave as expected; I cannot say the same thing
  for the average piece of software." (lightly normalized from the note)
- "What is this quality thing really, and what does it mean in the world of
  software, the world of things that do not really exist?"
