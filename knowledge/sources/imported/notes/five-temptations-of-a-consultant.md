# The Five Temptations of a Consultant (and the Consultant's Oath)

## Core argument

Consulting failure modes come in *pairs of opposite temptations* — the failure
is not a vice but a polarity managed badly. Riffing on Lencioni's "five
temptations" format, the talk names the polarities; the companion
"Consultant's Oath" ideas state the positive obligation: an external expert
should leave no legacy behind and should build the client's capability rather
than displace it.

The five temptations (each a polarity, both poles are failures):

1. **Show-off or impostor** — flaunting superior skill vs. hiding behind
   false modesty; corollary: choosing invulnerability over trust.
2. **Become the team or disconnect from the team** — going native (becoming
   the customer) vs. staying a detached outsider.
3. **Hands-on vs. hands-off / show vs. tell.**
4. **Solve the customer's problem** — the seductive one: solving it *for*
   them instead of building their ability to solve it.
5. **Curling consultant vs. laissez-faire** — sweeping every obstacle away
   ahead of the client vs. abandoning them; the ivory tower vs. dirty hands.

The oath side (from the "living the Consultant's Oath" drafts):

- Don't be a white-knight consultant: "a consultant shouldn't swoop in as a
  white knight in shining armor and save the poor damsel in distress. We and
  our customers are equals with a different perspective."
- Leave no legacy code behind: treat everything as open source — a newcomer
  armed only with the README must be able to build and test the code, add a
  trivial feature, deliver it through the pipeline, and follow the project's
  conventions. Leave a pipeline "such that you can always release." Hand
  over; make tacit knowledge explicit; don't be clever.

## Extracted claims

- Definitional: consultant failure modes are polarities, not vices — each
  temptation has a mirror-image failure, so "avoid X" advice creates the
  opposite failure.
- Normative: the consultant's product is client capability, not solved
  problems.
- Normative: the newcomer-with-README test is the operational bar for "no
  legacy left behind."
- Normative: consultant and client are epistemic equals with different
  perspectives — the value is the perspective difference, not superiority.

## Agreements and disagreements

Generalizes past consulting: the same polarity structure applies to platform
teams, staff engineers, enablement teams — any role whose success is measured
by what others can do without them. Tension candidate: "solve the customer's
problem" is what the customer is paying for; refusing to can be its own ivory
tower (the framework should treat this as a genuine tension, not a solved
question).

- "We and our customers are equals with a different perspective."
