# Automation is hard, and we are doing it wrong

## Core argument

Automation is touted as the magic bullet to productivity — infrastructure,
testing, deployment, traceability alike — and it is one of the pillars of
DevOps. Yet organizations spend large amounts of energy on automation and
struggle to reap the benefits. Automation is difficult and typically
approached badly: as a silver-bullet purchase rather than as an engineering
discipline with a mindset behind it.

## Extracted claims

- Empirical: many projects that start an automation effort "in search of the
  silver bullet" spend a lot of effort for low return on investment.
- Empirical: the failure is systematic, not incidental — there are recurring
  *ways* organizations go wrong in automation efforts (the talk's subject),
  implying the failure mode is in the approach, not the tools.
- Definitional: automation is an engineering activity — it has design,
  maintenance cost, and failure modes of its own — not a one-time productivity
  purchase.
- Normative: move "in the right direction with the right mindset" — strategy
  and mindset precede tooling; automate deliberately rather than maximally.

## Agreements and disagreements

Pairs with the SRE toil note (toil elimination as *the* engineering
investment) and tensions productively with it: SRE says automate relentlessly
to cap toil; this talk says undirected automation is where effort goes to die.
The synthesis candidate for the framework: automation ROI depends on what
systemic constraint it relieves, not on the amount automated. Also connects to
"employee time is free" (13 things) — organizations that don't price manual
work can't evaluate automation ROI at all.
