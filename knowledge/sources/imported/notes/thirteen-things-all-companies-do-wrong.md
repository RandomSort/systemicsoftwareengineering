# 13 things all companies do wrong

## Core argument

Against common sense — and defying knowledge freely available on Stack
Overflow — companies make the same mistakes over and over again. The
challenges any given organization faces are neither unique nor impossible to
overcome; the pathologies are so recurrent they can be cataloged. The talk's
stance: recognizing your dysfunction in a generic list is itself the
intervention, because the local narrative is always "our situation is
special."

## Extracted claims

The catalog (each a claimed near-universal pathology; empirical, from repeated
consulting observation):

1. **Employee time is treated as free** — tooling/process waste is invisible
   because salaries are sunk cost.
2. **Old tools** — running long-obsolete platforms (the note's example: an
   outdated OS years past its era).
3. **Publicly known service users** — shared credentials everyone knows.
4. **Never deprecating** — accreting compilers and source variants forever.
5. **Architecture is never prioritized** — always an afterthought; "we don't
   _need_ to do this, even though it might be nice."
6. **Not reacting to "this is just how it is here"** — normalized dysfunction,
   including employees joking that people leave for better jobs all the time.
7. **Building up management inventory** — unfinished decisions and initiatives
   pile up as inventory, invisible because it isn't physical.
8. **Reorganizing all the time.**
9. **No traceability in management** — organizations demand rigorous
   traceability from their technical departments while management decisions,
   from the average developer's perspective, have no visibility or
   traceability at all.
10. **Unable to make small changes** — batch size of organizational change is
    structurally large.
11. **"They're just not doing it right / don't understand" mindset** — blaming
    users/adopters instead of examining the system.
12. **Accepting a world of alternative facts** — internal claims nobody
    believes but everyone repeats.
13. **"We know Git, we had a training two years ago"** — treating a training
    event as durable capability. Punchline: "I know karate, I took a class two
    years ago."

Definitional: dysfunction that is joked about is dysfunction that has been
normalized — the joke is the diagnostic.

Normative: the response to "this is just how it is here" should be alarm, not
acceptance.

## Agreements and disagreements

Strongly systemic in spirit: pathologies live in structures (funding,
deprecation policy, change batch size), not in individuals. The management
traceability asymmetry (claim 9) is an unusually sharp observation — the
organization applies engineering discipline selectively, downward only. A
framework should test whether "all companies" is rhetorical or a real
empirical regularity, and whether the 13 reduce to fewer root causes (e.g.
invisible inventory + normalized dysfunction + asymmetric accountability).

## Quotable

- "I know karate, I took a class two years ago."
- "Not reacting to 'this is just how it is here'."
