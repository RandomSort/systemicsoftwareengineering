# The Agile Alignment Trap

## Core argument

Agile transformations are supposed to make the business more effective, with
happier developers and satisfied users — yet the phrase "agile transformation"
fills employees with dread. The talk covers research into why agile
transformations fail and argues that our intuition about the cure is wrong:
armed with the actual failure research, transformations can be approached
differently.

## Extracted claims

- Empirical: agile transformations fail at high rates, and employees'
  dread of them is a rational response to that track record.
- Empirical (via the "alignment trap" research the title references —
  Shpilberg et al., *MIT Sloan Management Review*, 2007): organizations that
  pursue business–IT *alignment* while IT is still *ineffective* perform worse
  than organizations that fix effectiveness first. Alignment amplifies
  whatever capability exists — including dysfunction. (Verify the citation
  and effect when fact-checking; the vault note names the trap but not the
  paper.)
- Definitional: "alignment" (doing what the business asks) and
  "effectiveness" (doing it well) are independent axes; conflating them is
  the trap.
- Normative: sequence matters — build execution capability before tightening
  alignment; intuition says the reverse, which is why transformations follow
  the failing path.

## Agreements and disagreements

Directly framework-relevant: it is a claim about the *order* in which systemic
properties must be improved, and about intuition being anti-correlated with
the right intervention. Candidate tension with transformation approaches that
lead with restructuring toward business outcomes (e.g. big-bang re-orgs,
value-stream reorganizations) before engineering capability exists.

## Quotable

- "Yet uttering the phrase Agile Transformation fills employees with dread."
