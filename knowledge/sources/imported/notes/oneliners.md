# One-liners — collected aphorisms

## Core argument

A curated collection of aphorisms the author gathered from talks, posts,
books, and colleagues — each a compressed heuristic about how software
organizations actually behave. Collectively they sketch a worldview:
existence and truth in a software organization are defined by its systems
(version control, monitoring, automation), organizational structure mirrors
process, and efficiency without direction is waste.

## Extracted claims

Existence-by-system heuristics:

- "If it's not under version control it does not exist" (via Troy Hunt's
  source-control commandments).
- "If it's not monitored it's not in production" (via Kris Buytaert).
- "If it is not automated, I can't remember it" (attributed to a colleague of
  the author, embedded-industry context).
- "A software project is not done until your last end-user is in the grave"
  (via Kris Buytaert) — lifecycle, not delivery.
- "If my computer can't install it, the installer is broken" (Luke Kanies,
  FOSDEM 2007) — blame the artifact, not the user (cf. the #NoDocs note).

Structure and change:

- "Change the process → you must change the organization. Your organization
  is structured to support your existing process" — Conway-adjacent, stated
  as a constraint on transformation.
- "If you want to go fast go alone, if you want to go far go in a group" —
  with the author's gloss: lasting change is a paradigm/habit change, "it's
  kind of like a diet" — one intense week doesn't work.
- "Requirements gathering doesn't work because the requirements you'll gather
  are wrong."
- "If I need training I need training now. If I need software, I need it
  now" — demand for capability is not schedulable.

Direction over efficiency:

- "There is nothing so useless as doing efficiently that which should not be
  done at all" (Peter Drucker).
- "Why be agile when you can be efficient" — kept as an ironic trap.
- "Don't *do* Agile, *be* agile."
- "What gets measured gets managed" — kept alongside the abused-figures
  skepticism.
- "Care more about less" (attributed).
- "You should listen to the users, not obey them" (via *The Inmates Are
  Running the Asylum*).
- "You are special, you are unique, it just doesn't matter" — against
  our-context-is-different exceptionalism (cf. "13 things": the problems are
  not unique).
- "If you're up to the neck in alligators, it can be hard to remember that
  you set out to drain the swamp."
- "Defer manual processes as long as possible."
- "Don't do white knight consulting."
- "I disagree with you and I love [it/you]" — disagreement as a bonding move.

## Agreements and disagreements

The collection is internally productive: "what gets measured gets managed"
sits unresolved next to the author's measurement skepticism, and "be
efficient" next to Drucker. These pairs are pre-made tension notes. The
existence-by-system cluster is the most framework-ready: it defines an
organization's *effective reality* as whatever its automated systems can see.
