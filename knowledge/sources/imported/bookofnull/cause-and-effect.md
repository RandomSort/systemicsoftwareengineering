# Cause and Effect

Source piece: `bookofnull/pieces/004-cause-and-effect.md` (three-line koan).

## Core argument

A three-way contrast about causal reasoning. We *believe* our
cause-and-effect inferences are the strongest link in our chain of
reasoning; in our minds they are actually the weakest (we habitually infer
causes from correlation, narrative, and hindsight); yet in reality,
cause-and-effect is the *only* link — the system itself runs on nothing but
mechanism. The gap between the reliability we assign to our causal models
and their actual reliability is where most engineering surprise lives.

## Extracted claims

- **Empirical:** humans systematically overestimate the reliability of
  their causal explanations (consistent with research on the illusion of
  explanatory depth, hindsight bias, and narrative fallacy — candidates for
  /fact-check and /enrich).
- **Definitional:** in the running system there are no narratives, blame,
  or intentions — only mechanism. All non-causal explanation is a property
  of the observer, not the system.
- **Normative:** treat every causal explanation (especially in incident
  analysis) as a hypothesis requiring a test, not as a finding.

## Framework relevance

A strong seed for a core "causal humility" concept in a systemic framework:
systems thinking is largely the discipline of repairing weak causal models
(feedback loops, delays, non-linearity are exactly where naive causal
inference fails). Connects to *Intelligence* (040, hindsight bias around
bugs) and to critiques of root-cause analysis / five-whys. Pairs with
*Test your Assumptions* (038) as the remedy side.

## Quotable

- "Inside our minds it is the weakest / In reality it is the only"
