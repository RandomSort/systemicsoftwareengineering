# On Faith

Source piece: `bookofnull/pieces/035-on-faith.md` (eight-line litany).

## Core argument

An enumeration of the engineer's daily epistemic states — "I can't believe
this doesn't work", "I can't believe this works", "I can't believe this is
how it works", and their negations — resolving into "I believe this should
work / I have faith". The exhaustive inflection shows that the working
vocabulary of engineering is belief vocabulary: most of what an engineer
"knows" about a system is unverified conviction, held until reality
contradicts it. The closing line names the condition honestly: much of
software practice runs on faith.

## Extracted claims

- **Empirical:** engineers routinely act on untested beliefs about system
  behavior, and express surprise (in both directions) when systems meet
  reality — i.e., their mental models are systematically miscalibrated.
- **Definitional:** "it should work" is a statement about the speaker's
  model, not about the system; surprise is the measurement of model error.
- **Normative:** faith is unavoidable (systems exceed comprehension) but
  should be budgeted — convert belief to knowledge where verification is
  cheap, and know where the remaining faith lives.

## Framework relevance

Pairs with *Cause and Effect* (004, weak causal models) and *Test your
Assumptions* (038, verification's limits) to form an epistemics cluster:
systemic engineering as the management of a portfolio of beliefs of varying
confidence. Directly resonates with this repo's own convention that
"confidence is data". Also touches trust in abstractions — every dependency
used without reading it is an act of faith, mostly justified.

## Quotable

- "I believe this should work / I have faith"
